Pulse - The ASC Growth Play

This caselet works through a surgical-centre chain's growth plan using a simple four-step framework: understand the growth objective, evaluate new-city expansion, identify organic growth at existing centres, and recommend a plan with its risks.

When no single lever reaches the target, look for a combination. Focus on what each lever contributes, and whether the revenue is also profitable.

Understand the Objective → Evaluate Expansion → Find Organic Levers → Recommend and De-risk
  • What is the growth target? Define the revenue goal, the time frame and the profitability constraint, and break revenue into its drivers.
  • Where can the revenue come from? Compare candidate cities on doctor availability and specialty mix, then size the opportunity within existing centres.
  • Is the plan viable? Combine the levers against the target and test the plan against investment, ramp-up, competition and margin risks.

ConceptWhat you learnt
Revenue Growth DriversRevenue can grow through higher customer volumes, additional services, improved utilisation and expansion into new markets. Understanding the underlying drivers helps identify practical growth opportunities.
Market and Location SelectionChoosing a new market means evaluating resource availability, demand, competitive intensity and revenue potential. A larger market is not necessarily the most attractive one.
Capacity UtilisationMeasures how effectively available resources are used. Improving utilisation at existing facilities can generate additional revenue without equivalent investment in new infrastructure.
Revenue MixThe contribution of different products, services or specialties to total revenue. Focusing on higher-reimbursement services can improve revenue potential, although profitability also depends on the associated costs.
Portfolio Approach to GrowthCombining multiple growth initiatives instead of relying on a single opportunity. This reduces dependence on one initiative, provided each is independently feasible and the combined economics are sound.
